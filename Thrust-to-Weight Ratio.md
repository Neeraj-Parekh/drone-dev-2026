### Thrust-to-Weight Ratio (TWR) Definition

The **Thrust-to-Weight Ratio** ($TWR$) is a dimensionless parameter indicating the ratio of the maximum total thrust generated by an aircraft's propulsion system to its total gravitational force at maximum take-off weight (MTOW). In multirotor design, it determines the vehicle's control authority, agility, stability margin under external aerodynamic disturbances, and vertical acceleration capability.

### 1. Mathematical & Aerodynamic Formulations

#### Symmetrical Multirotor TWR Formula

For a hexacopter ($N=6$), the overall system $TWR$ is calculated as the sum of all individual rotor thrust capacities divided by the total loaded weight:

$$TWR = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{6} T_{rotor\_max}}{MTOW \cdot g}$$

Where:

- $T_{rotor\_max}$: The maximum thrust generated by a single motor-propeller assembly at $100\%$ throttle at nominal system voltage (in Newtons, $\text{N}$).
    
- $MTOW$: Maximum Take-Off Weight (in kilograms, $\text{kg}$).
    
- $g$: Acceleration due to gravity ($9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$).
    

#### Dynamic Throttle Allocation for Hover

To maintain a stable, unaccelerated static hover, the total upward thrust must exactly equal the gravitational force ($TWR_{hover} = 1.0$). The percentage of maximum throttle required to hover ($\%_{hover}$) is inversely proportional to the maximum $TWR$:

$$\%_{hover} = \frac{1}{TWR} \times 100\%$$

- **If $TWR = 2.0$:** Hover occurs at exactly $50\%$ throttle. This is the industrial baseline for multirotors. It provides an identical $50\%$ throttle margin for attitudinal correction, climbing maneuvers, and gust rejection.
    
- **If $TWR < 1.5$:** The aircraft will hover at $>66\%$ throttle. This leaves less than $34\%$ control authority for stabilization. The motors will run hot, transient voltage drops will occur, and the drone will struggle to recover from downwashes or payload sloshing.
    

#### Available Vertical Acceleration ($a_z$)

The maximum vertical acceleration an aircraft can command from a static hover state is directly limited by its maximum $TWR$:

$$a_z = g \cdot (TWR - 1)$$
