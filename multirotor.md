### Multirotor Aerodynamics & Flight Dynamics Definition

A multirotor is an uncrewed rotorcraft utilizing three or more equidistant vertical lift rotors to achieve omnidirectional vector flight. Unlike traditional helicopters, multirotors employ fixed-pitch blades where all translational and rotational maneuvers are controlled strictly through independent variations of individual motor angular velocities (RPM).

### 1. Rigorous Aerodynamic Matrix & Mechanical Physics Formulations

#### Lift-to-Weight Balance & Vertical Acceleration

To alter vertical position (climb or descend), the total vertical thrust component ($T_{vertical}$) must exceed or fall below the downward gravitational force ($MTOW \cdot g$).

$$\sum_{i=1}^{N} T_i = MTOW \cdot (g + a_z)$$

Where:

- $N$: Total number of rotors (for our target system, $N = 6$ rotors).
    
- $T_i$: Thrust vector produced by individual rotor $i$ (in Newtons, $\text{N}$).
    
- $a_z$: Vertical acceleration component ($\text{m/s}^2$). When $a_z = 0$, the system is in static hover.
    

#### Angular Momentum & Moment Conservation (Yaw Control)

Yaw torque ($\tau_{yaw}$) is generated by the mismatch of aerodynamic reactive torque between clockwise (CW) and counter-clockwise (CCW) rotating propellers.

$$\tau_{yaw} = \sum \tau_{CW} - \sum \tau_{CCW}$$

- **Clockwise Rotors (Group A):** Generate a counter-clockwise torque on the airframe structure.
    
- **Counter-Clockwise Rotors (Group B):** Generate a clockwise torque on the airframe structure.
    
- To execute a pure yaw rotation with zero translational shift, the flight controller increases the RPM of Group A while identically decreasing the RPM of Group B. Total system thrust remains constant ($T_{total} = MTOW \cdot g$).
    

#### Attitudinal Changes (Pitch and Roll Control)

Translational lateral movement is achieved by creating an asymmetric thrust distribution across the frame geometry, producing a net torque about the center of mass ($CoM$).

$$\tau_{roll} = \sum (T_{left} \cdot d_{left}) - \sum (T_{right} \cdot d_{right})$$

$$\tau_{pitch} = \sum (T_{rear} \cdot d_{rear}) - \sum (T_{front} \cdot d_{front})$$

Where:

- $d$: The perpendicular moment arm distance from the rotor axis of rotation to the aircraft center of mass ($CoM$).
    
- The resulting angular tilt tips the total thrust vector, breaking it into a horizontal translation vector ($T \cdot \sin\theta$) and a vertical holding vector ($T \cdot \cos\theta$).

### 3. Hexacopter Rotor Control Mapping Diagram

For a standard symmetrical Hexa-X operational configuration, the flight controller varies motor velocities based on this structural matrix to achieve stability and multi-axis movement:

```
                  [ FRONT OF AIRCRAFT ]
                    Rotor 1 (CCW) ↑
                         \     /
                          \   /
      Rotor 5 (CW) <-------   -------> Rotor 2 (CW)
                           | |
                           | |  (Center of Mass)
      Rotor 4 (CCW) <------   ------> Rotor 3 (CCW)
                          /   \
                         /     \
                    Rotor 6 (CW) ↓
```

- **To Pitch Forward:** Accelerate Rotors [4, 6, 3]; Decelerate Rotors [1, 5, 2].
    
- **To Roll Right:** Accelerate Rotors [5, 4]; Decelerate Rotors [2, 3].
    
- **To Yaw Clockwise:** Accelerate CCW Rotors [1, 3, 4]; Decelerate CW Rotors [2, 5, 6].
